# SBX-H ex-post remuneration architecture

Status: accepted rollback architecture after the automatic-restoration
validation of 2026-07-14.

## Scope

SBX-H contains two time-separated functions only:

1. Planning establishes the bilateral corridor-terminal voltage schedule.
2. Operation meters the resulting terminal voltages and reactive flows and
   remunerates useful physical support ex post.

There are no real-time support requests, offers, negotiations, grants,
temporary voltage commands, or strength products.

For the current experiment campaign, every area communicates the constant
planning reference

\[
V^{plan}_{i}(t)=1.03\;\mathrm{pu}\qquad \forall i\in\{1,2,3\},\;\forall t.
\]

Consequently, experiment 014 has no planning-schedule change. The general
contract representation may still accept externally prepared planning-phase
schedules in future experiments.

## Operational mechanism

For each corridor `(A,B)` and settlement window:

1. Both sides track their active planning references with their existing local
   TSO controllers. The controller actuators remain generator/DER reactive
   power, OLTCs, and shunt compensation, using cached models/sensitivities.
2. The bilateral line model evaluates the counterfactual reactive baseline
   `Q_0` at the planned terminal voltages and measured active transfer.
3. Each side is classified independently:
   - hold: every own terminal is within the 2.5 mpu holding tolerance;
   - violation: at least one own terminal is more than 5 mpu below or above its
     planning reference;
   - transition: neither condition.
4. Payment is possible only if exactly one side violates and the other holds.
5. The signed residual `dQ = Q_meas - Q_0` must have the direction that relieves
   the violating side.
6. The first `B_Q = 10 Mvar` of residual is a no-payment deadband. The remainder
   is integrated over the metering interval and paid at the configured
   `EUR/(Mvar h)` price.
7. Persistent area violations or beyond-band residuals may raise a slow
   re-planning indicator, but never change a runtime setpoint.

This is remuneration for what the coupled AC physics and independently acting
controllers actually deliver. It is not a promise of a pre-agreed Q quantity.

## Controlled outputs and constraints

- Local TSO controlled outputs: monitored EHV voltages, currents, generator-Q,
  and underlying-interface Q targets already present in the area controller.
- SBX-H metered outputs: corridor-terminal voltage, active transfer, and
  reactive transfer.
- Security constraints remain local voltage, current, capability, and actuator
  constraints. SBX-H settlement does not override them.
- No controller sees the plant directly; control continues through cached
  sensitivities/models and measured outputs.

## Interpretation limits

- A payment demonstrates coincident useful physical support under the stated
  counterfactual model; it does not establish causality or reserve capacity.
- `B_Q` is a commercial/noise deadband, not a physical capability interval.
- Constant 1.03 pu is an experiment assumption, not a universal optimal planning
  voltage.
- Different planning references may be studied later only as explicit
  planning-phase scenarios, not generated by runtime coordination.
